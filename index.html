Research

An anomaly is not always visible one observation at a time. A loaded die looks fair on any single throw and gives itself away only in the frequencies of many — and the same holds for new fundamental interactions distorting the distributions of particle collisions, or for a language model whose behaviour has drifted in ways no single output reveals. Classical tests answer that question rigorously but lose power when deviations are rare or buried among uninformative features. I build tests that scale without giving up the calibrated p-value or the account of what produced it.
